# Unemployment Insurance & Workers' Compensation Benefits Rules

## Unemployment Insurance (UI) — Overview

UI provides temporary income support to workers who lose their jobs through no fault of their own. Funded by employer payroll taxes (FUTA) and state taxes. Benefit amounts and durations vary by state.

---

## UI Eligibility & Disqualifications

### Eligible Reasons for Job Loss
1. **Layoff / Reduction in Force (RIF)**
   - Employer initiated, not employee fault
   - **Eligibility:** APPROVED (no disqualifying factors)
   - **Typical duration:** 12–26 weeks

2. **Temporary Layoff**
   - Employer expects to recall within specified time (e.g., 4 weeks)
   - **Eligibility:** APPROVED initially; stop benefits if recalled
   - **Note:** Claimant must report recall date and return to work

3. **Illness / Temporary Inability to Work**
   - Employee hospitalized or under doctor's orders not to work
   - **Eligibility:** INELIGIBLE for UI (refer to short-term disability or FMLA)
   - **Note:** Not considered work-related for UI purposes

### Ineligible Reasons for Job Loss
1. **Voluntary Quit Without Good Cause**
   - Employee resigned or terminated by mutual agreement
   - **Eligibility:** INELIGIBLE (deny first 1–4 weeks depending on state; then re-evaluate)
   - **Good cause examples:** Unsafe working conditions, significant wage reduction, relocation (family emergency)
   - **No good cause examples:** "Found another job," "Personal reasons," "Wanted to try something else"
   - **Burden of proof:** On claimant to demonstrate good cause

2. **Discharge for Misconduct**
   - Employer terminated for willful or deliberate rule violation
   - **Eligibility:** INELIGIBLE (deny first 1–4 weeks; then re-evaluate)
   - **Misconduct defined:** Willful violation of employer rules; NOT mere negligence or poor performance
   - **Misconduct examples:**
     - Stealing, fighting, insubordination, repeated violations after warnings
   - **NOT misconduct:**
     - Poor performance, honest mistake, inability to meet expectations, conflict with boss
   - **Burden of proof:** On employer to demonstrate willful misconduct

3. **Refusal of Suitable Work**
   - Employee refused offer of suitable employment (UI worker or new employer)
   - **Eligibility:** INELIGIBLE (deny current week + following week)
   - **Definition of "suitable":** Similar wage, hours, working conditions as prior employment (within 10–20% wage)

4. **Failure to Report / Missed Interview**
   - Employee missed required check-in, failed to report for job interview, or didn't maintain contact
   - **Eligibility:** INELIGIBLE (deny 1 week)

### Disqualifying Factors (Permanent or Extended Denials)
- **Fraud:** Knowingly providing false information to obtain UI
- **Unreported earnings:** Failing to report earnings while receiving UI (results in overpayment recovery)
- **Quid pro quo violation:** Accepting payment to give up UI claim or rights

## UI Benefit Calculation

### Weekly Benefit Amount (WBA)

**Standard formula:**
```
WBA = (Total earnings, past 4–8 weeks) / Number of weeks × Replacement ratio
```

**Typical replacement ratio:** 50% of prior earnings (varies by state; range 40–66%)

**Example:**
- Prior 4 weeks earnings: $480, $520, $490, $510 = $2,000 total
- Average weekly: $2,000 / 4 = $500
- Weekly benefit: $500 × 50% = **$250/week**

### Weekly Benefit Cap
- Varies by state: typically $300–$600/week
- Example: If calculated benefit = $450 but state cap = $400, claimant receives **$400/week**

### Maximum Duration
- **Standard:** 12–26 weeks (varies by state)
- **Extended:** 39–52 weeks (during high unemployment periods; requires federal approval)
- **Emergency periods:** Up to 99 weeks (during recessions; requires federal/state extension)

### Partial Unemployment (Reduced Hours)
- **Eligibility:** Claimant still employed but hours reduced
- **Weekly benefit:** (Normal WBA) − (Earnings over threshold)
- **Earnings limit:** Typically $150–300/week (varies by state)

**Example:**
- Normal weekly benefit: $300
- Claimant earned $200 that week
- Earnings threshold: $300/week
- Benefit: $300 − ($200 − $300) = $300 (full benefit because earnings < threshold)

## Workers' Compensation (WC) — Overview

WC provides benefits to workers injured on the job or who develop occupational diseases. Funded by employer insurance (varies by state). Benefits include:
1. Medical benefits (100% of reasonable treatment)
2. Wage loss benefits (% of pre-injury wage based on disability)
3. Vocational rehabilitation (retraining if employee cannot return to prior job)
4. Permanent disability (if injury results in permanent impairment)

---

## WC Eligibility & Coverage

### Eligible Work-Related Injuries
1. **Acute Traumatic Injury**
   - Sudden injury with specific date, time, location
   - **Examples:**
     - Fall from height, motor vehicle accident, machinery crush, cut/burn, overexertion injury
   - **Eligibility:** APPROVED (if no disqualifying factors)
   - **Medical coverage:** 100% of reasonable and necessary treatment
   - **Wage loss:** (Pre-injury avg. weekly wage) × disability percentage
   - **Typical duration:** Until medical maximum improvement (MMI) or 2–3 years, depending on state

2. **Occupational Disease (Cumulative Exposure)**
   - Disease caused by prolonged exposure during employment
   - **Examples:**
     - Repetitive strain injury (RSI): Carpal tunnel from keyboard work, tendinitis from assembly line
     - Noise-induced hearing loss (NIHL): Prolonged exposure to noise >85 dB over 10+ years
     - Chemical exposure: Asbestos mesothelioma, lead poisoning, solvent-induced neuropathy
     - Infectious disease: Healthcare worker exposure to bloodborne pathogens, TB
     - Stress-induced: Occupational stress disorder (varies by state; rare)
   - **Latency rule:** Disease must manifest within 1–5 years of exposure (varies by state)
   - **Eligibility:** APPROVED if work-related causation clear (latency within acceptable timeframe)
   - **Medical coverage:** 100% of treatment
   - **Wage loss:** Same calculation as acute injury

3. **Aggravation of Pre-Existing Condition**
   - Work-related worsening of prior condition (NOT the baseline pre-existing condition)
   - **Examples:**
     - Claimant had mild back pain; work injury causes severe herniated disc
     - Claimant has diabetes; work injury prevents mobility, worsens management
     - Claimant had prior ankle sprain; re-injury at work causes worse sprain
   - **Distinction:** WC covers "worsening," NOT the pre-existing baseline
   - **Eligibility:** APPROVED for worsening portion only
   - **Medical coverage:** 100% for treatment related to worsening
   - **Wage loss:** Calculated based on additional disability from worsening only

4. **Mental Health / Psychiatric Injury (Limited)**
   - **Most states DO NOT cover pure mental health claims**
   - **Exceptions:**
     - Mental health caused by physical injury (e.g., PTSD from severe burn injury)
     - Physical manifestation of mental health (e.g., stress-induced cardiac event)
   - **Examples NOT covered:**
     - Job stress, workplace bullying, termination-induced anxiety, manager conflict
   - **Eligibility:** GENERALLY DENIED unless physical injury component present

### Non-Eligible / Ineligible Work-Related Claims
- **Illness from non-work source:** COVID-19 unrelated to healthcare work (varies by state)
- **Injury from voluntary participation in sports/recreation:** Employee gym injury on lunch break
- **Injury from severe violation of safety rules:** Despite warnings about hazard
- **Injury during illegal activity:** Employee injured while committing theft, drug use

---

## WC Disqualifications (Permanent Denials)

1. **Injury Caused by Willful Misconduct**
   - Employee deliberately violated safety rules or engaged in dangerous behavior
   - **Examples:** Ignoring "Wet Floor" sign and slipping; climbing without safety harness after being told not to
   - **Eligibility:** DENIED entirely (no coverage)

2. **Injury Caused by Intoxication or Drug Use**
   - Injury occurred while employee was under influence of alcohol/drugs
   - **Examples:** Employee drunk on the job; injured due to impaired judgment
   - **Eligibility:** DENIED entirely (no coverage)
   - **Note:** Employer must prove causation (intoxication caused injury)

3. **Injury Caused by Horseplay or Fighting**
   - Employee injured during non-work-related horseplay or altercation
   - **Examples:** Coworkers wrestling, fistfight over personal matter
   - **Eligibility:** DENIED entirely (no coverage)

4. **Injury Not Reported Timely**
   - Employee fails to report injury within required timeframe (30–60 days, varies by state)
   - **Eligibility:** DENIED entire claim (inability to investigate)
   - **Exception:** Latent injury that was not immediately apparent may have extended reporting window

## WC Benefit Calculation

### Medical Benefits
- **Coverage:** 100% of reasonable and necessary treatment
- **Includes:** Doctor visits, surgery, hospitalization, physical therapy, medications, medical devices (braces, crutches)
- **Excludes:** Cosmetic surgery, non-work-related treatment, experimental treatment not approved by state
- **Cap:** Some states have caps on total lifetime medical; most states do NOT cap medical benefits

### Wage Loss Benefits (Temporary Total Disability)

**Formula:**
```
Weekly wage loss benefit = Pre-injury average weekly wage × (% disability / 100)
```

**Disability percentages:**
- 100% disability (unable to work): Full pre-injury wage (reduced by fee)
- 75% disability (significant limitations): 75% pre-injury wage
- 50% disability (moderate limitations): 50% pre-injury wage
- 25% disability (minor limitations): 25% pre-injury wage

**Example:**
- Pre-injury weekly wage: $600
- Physician determines 75% temporary disability
- Weekly benefit: $600 × 0.75 = **$450/week**
- Duration: Until medical improvement or return to work

### Wage Loss Cap
- Varies by state: typically 66% of average state weekly wage (approx. $500–800/week)
- Some states cap at 1× pre-injury wage; others at 2/3

### Permanent Disability (Impairment Rating)

**Applicable when:** Employee reaches medical maximum improvement (MMI) but still has permanent limitations

**Formula:**
```
Permanent disability benefit = Impairment % × Pre-injury weekly wage × Weeks factor
```

**Weeks factor:** Varies by body part (e.g., 300 weeks for hand loss, 150 weeks for ankle injury)

**Example:**
- Impairment: 25% (based on physician exam and state rating schedule)
- Body part: Ankle
- Weeks factor: 150 weeks
- Pre-injury wage: $600
- Permanent disability lump sum: 0.25 × $600 × 150 = **$22,500**

### Vocational Rehabilitation
- **Eligibility:** Employee unable to return to prior job due to injury
- **Benefit:** Retraining costs (tuition, books), job counseling, wage subsidy during retraining
- **Typical duration:** 1–2 years

## Federal & State Variations

### Federal Guidance (USDOL)
- Employer must post OSHA 300 log (injury/illness records)
- Employee has right to see OSHA log and injury/illness information
- Employer must provide written notice of injury rights within specified timeframe

### State Variations (Examples)

**California:**
- Temporary disability: 66⅔% of pre-injury wage; cap approx. $1,400/week
- Permanent disability: Detailed rating schedule; lifetime benefits possible
- Return-to-work bonuses available to employers

**Texas:**
- WC coverage is optional for employers (non-subscribers possible)
- No cap on temporary disability; extends until MMI
- Permanent disability: Impairment rating multiplied by wage replacement factor

**New York:**
- Temporary disability: 66⅔% of pre-injury wage; cap approx. $600/week
- Supplemental job disability: Available if return-to-work wages lower than pre-injury
- Schedule awards: Specific body parts have fixed benefit amounts (e.g., arm = 312 weeks)

## Overpayment Detection & Recovery

### Common Overpayment Scenarios

1. **Double-Income Weeks (UI)**
   - Claimant worked AND received UI in same week
   - **Recovery:** 100% of UI benefit paid that week

2. **Unreported Earnings**
   - Claimant failed to report earnings to state
   - **Recovery:** 100% of overpaid amount + interest (varies by state; 10% annually typical)

3. **Returned-to-Work Not Reported**
   - Claimant resumed employment but continued claiming UI
   - **Recovery:** All benefits paid after return date + potential fraud penalties (25–50% additional)

### Overpayment Recovery Mechanisms

- **Offset from future benefits:** 10% withhold from ongoing UI or WC checks
- **Lump-sum demand:** State issues bill for full overpayment amount; typically 30–60 day payment window
- **Payment plan:** If unable to pay lump sum, establish monthly payment arrangement (10% withhold)
- **Employer restitution:** If employer at fault (e.g., failed to report wages), employer may reimburse state

## Processing Timelines (Typical)

| Decision Type | Timeline | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Auto-Approve (UI/WC) | 2–5 business days | Requires all docs present; clear eligibility |
| Escalation Review | 10–15 business days | Requires hearing officer or manual review |
| Hearing Officer Decision | 30–60 days | After claimant appeal filed; may extend if discovery needed |
| WC Determination (acute injury) | 5–10 business days | Medical records available; clear work-relatedness |
| WC Determination (occupational disease) | 30–90 days | May require specialist medical opinions; causation review |
| Overpayment Recovery Notice | 30 days | After decision issued; 30-day appeal window |

## Multi-Language Support Notes

For claimants in Spanish, Vietnamese, Hmong, Somali, etc.:
- Provide claim forms in claimant's primary language
- Conduct appeal hearings with certified interpreter
- Issue benefit notices in claimant language (bilingual requirement in many states)
- UI/WC website should offer language selection

---

## Compliance & Audit Requirements

- **USDOL Audit Trail:** Maintain records of all decisions, reasoning, evidence for 3–5 years
- **OASIS reporting:** States report quarterly benefit data to USDOL
- **Experience rating:** Employers' UI tax rates based on claim history
- **Fraud investigation:** State audits random claims and matches income data (IRS, state employers)
