# State Licensure Requirements Reference

## Medical License (MD/DO)

### Education Requirements
- **U.S./Canadian Medical School:** Degree from LCME-accredited MD program or AOA-accredited DO program (4-year degree minimum)
- **Foreign-Educated Physician:** Degree from WDOMS-accredited school + ECFMG certification (Educational Commission for Foreign Medical Graduates)
- **International Medical Graduate (IMG):** Pass ECFMG exam + obtain certification before USMLE candidacy

### Examination Requirements
- **USMLE (Allopathic):**
  - Step 1: Minimum score **230** (required for initial licensure)
  - Step 2 CK: Minimum score **230** (required for initial licensure)
  - Step 3: Minimum score **200** (required for independent practice)
  - 3-year passage window required (must pass all steps within 3 calendar years of Step 1)
  
- **COMLEX (Osteopathic):**
  - Level 1: Minimum score **400** (equivalent to USMLE Step 1)
  - Level 2: Minimum score **400** (equivalent to USMLE Step 2 CK)
  - Level 3: Minimum score **400** (equivalent to USMLE Step 3)

### Reciprocity & Interstate Agreements
- **National Medical Commission (NMC):** Facilitates recognition across 30+ states; requires USMLE or COMLEX scores + state exam waiver
- **Multistate Licensure:** No formal compact; each state requires separate application
- **Visa Sponsorship:** Visa holders must pass USMLE + visa screen process

### Disqualifying Factors
- Felony conviction (especially drug-related or crimes against persons)
- Prior license revocation or suspension
- Unresolved malpractice judgments (>$250k typically requires review)
- Substance abuse history (ongoing or unresolved)
- Disciplinary action in another state
- Failure to pay professional debts or fines

### Continuing Education
- **Annual requirement:** 40–50 CME hours (varies by state)
- **Maintenance of Licensure (MOL):** May be required annually or every 10 years depending on state

## Legal License (Juris Doctor)

### Education Requirements
- **U.S. Law School:** Juris Doctor from ABA-accredited law school (3-year degree minimum)
- **Foreign-Educated Attorney:** Bachelor of Laws (LL.B.) or Master of Laws (LL.M.) + NYLE (New York Law Exam) or QLTS (Qualified Lawyers Transfer Scheme) pathway
- **Law School Approval:** Graduation from school on ABA-approved list (minimum 1L GPA typically 2.0)

### Examination Requirements
- **Bar Exam:**
  - **UBE (Uniform Bar Exam):** Administered in 40+ states; score ≥ 270 (out of 400) minimum
  - **Non-UBE States:** State-specific bar exam; passing score varies by state (typically 60–70% correct)
  - **NYLE (New York Law Exam):** Score ≥ 80 (out of 100) for foreign-educated attorneys

### Reciprocity & Interstate Agreements
- **UBE Reciprocity:** Attorneys licensed in one UBE state can transfer to another with proof of good standing (UBE score from 2016 or later)
- **Foreign Attorney Reciprocity:** Limited; most states require full bar exam or NYLE
- **No national bar exam:** Each jurisdiction controls licensing independently

### Character & Fitness Review
- Criminal convictions (misdemeanor ≥ 1 year jail time or any felony requires review)
- Prior bar discipline or law license suspension/revocation
- Unpaid debts or judgments (especially client trust account violations)
- Dishonest conduct or fraud
- Substance abuse (if ongoing or unresolved)

### Disqualifying Factors
- Felony conviction (permanent disqualification in most states)
- Prior bar suspension or revocation
- Fraud or dishonesty findings by character & fitness committee
- Failure to disclose criminal history or prior discipline
- Unauthorized practice of law

### Continuing Legal Education (CLE)
- **Annual requirement:** 12–15 CLE hours (varies by state)
- **Ethics/Professionalism:** 1–3 hours must be professional responsibility
- **Exemptions:** Judicial officers, retired attorneys, military service (varies)

## Nursing License (RN/LPN)

### Education Requirements
- **RN (Registered Nurse):**
  - Bachelor of Science in Nursing (BSN) from CCNE-accredited program, OR
  - Associate Degree in Nursing (ADN) from ACEN-accredited program, OR
  - Diploma in Nursing from NLNAC-accredited program (increasingly rare)
  
- **LPN (Licensed Practical Nurse):**
  - Associate Degree in Practical Nursing or LPN certificate from ACEN or NLNAC-accredited program (12–18 months)
  
- **Foreign-Educated Nurse:**
  - CGFNS (Commission on Graduates of Foreign Nursing Schools) certification or NCEES (National Council of the State Boards of Nursing) examination pathway
  - English language proficiency test (TOEFL or IELTS) if educated outside U.S.

### Examination Requirements
- **NCLEX-RN (Registered Nurse):**
  - Minimum score: **205** (out of maximum variable score; typically 145–265 scale)
  - Computerized adaptive testing (CAT)
  - Pass rate: ~85% on first attempt nationally
  
- **NCLEX-PN (Licensed Practical Nurse):**
  - Minimum score: **163** (out of maximum variable score)
  - Computerized adaptive testing (CAT)
  - Pass rate: ~80% on first attempt nationally
  
- **CGFNS (for foreign-educated):**
  - Minimum score: **350** (out of 600); also requires TOEFL ≥ 575 or IELTS ≥ 6.5

### Reciprocity & Interstate Agreements
- **Multistate Nursing Compact:** 26 states + Washington D.C. allow licensure by endorsement (no additional exam required)
- **Non-Compact States:** Require separate application and state-specific requirements (some may require additional exam or fingerprinting)
- **Foreign-Educated:** CGFNS pathway accepted in most states; some have additional requirements (HIV testing, criminal background clearance)

### Disqualifying Factors
- Felony conviction (especially drug-related, patient abuse, or crimes against persons)
- Prior nursing license revocation or suspension
- Substance abuse (active or unresolved, especially opioid-related)
- Patient abuse or neglect findings
- Unprofessional conduct or fraud
- Failure to maintain required vaccinations (varies by state; COVID-19 vaccine may be required)

### Continuing Education (CE)
- **RN:** 20–50 CE hours per 2–3 years (varies by state)
- **LPN:** 20–40 CE hours per 2–3 years (varies by state)
- **Renewal:** Typically every 2–3 years; fee $50–150

## Contractor Licenses (HVAC, Electrical, Plumbing)

### Education & Apprenticeship Requirements

#### HVAC (Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning)
- **Apprenticeship:** 5,000 hours (often split 4,000 classroom + 1,000 on-the-job)
- **Alternative:** 2–4 years relevant work experience (varies by state)
- **Classroom hours:** 200–400 hours in HVAC theory, EPA certification, electrical basics

#### Electrical
- **Apprenticeship:** 8,000 hours (often split 1,000 classroom + 7,000 on-the-job)
- **Alternative:** 4–5 years relevant work experience (varies by state)
- **Classroom hours:** 400–600 hours in electrical theory, national electrical code (NEC), safety

#### Plumbing
- **Apprenticeship:** 5,000 hours (often split 900 classroom + 4,100 on-the-job)
- **Alternative:** 3–5 years relevant work experience (varies by state)
- **Classroom hours:** 300–500 hours in plumbing code, water systems, safety

### Examination Requirements
- **Journeyperson Exam:** Passing score ≥ **70%** (covers theory, code compliance, safety)
- **Master Exam (optional, for business ownership):** Passing score ≥ **75%** (covers business law, estimating, crew management)
- **Open-Book Permitted:** Most states allow code books and references during exam

### Reciprocity & Interstate Agreements
- **Limited reciprocity:** Most states require local exam and fingerprinting
- **Portable card programs:** Some states recognize certifications from HVACR and electrical training organizations
- **Temporary permits:** Some states issue temporary permits for out-of-state contractors on specific projects

### Disqualifying Factors
- Felony conviction (especially workplace safety violations)
- Prior license revocation or suspension
- Unpaid fines or judgments related to code violations
- Failure to maintain liability insurance or bonding
- Unsafe work practices or documented safety violations
- Failure to complete continuing education

### Continuing Education (CE)
- **HVAC:** 8–12 hours annually (often focused on new equipment, refrigerant regulations)
- **Electrical:** 8–24 hours every 3 years (focused on NEC updates, safety)
- **Plumbing:** 8–16 hours every 2–3 years (focused on code updates, water quality)
- **Renewal fee:** $100–300 annually; CE costs $200–500/year

## General Considerations for All License Types

### Background Check Requirements
- **FBI Criminal Fingerprinting:** Federal and state criminal history
- **National Practitioner Data Bank (NPDB):** For healthcare providers; checks malpractice history
- **License History:** Check in all states where applicant has held licenses
- **Credit Check:** May be required for business-owned licenses (contractors)

### Timeline Expectations
- **Routine approval:** 10–15 business days (after all documents received)
- **Escalation review:** 30–60 days (if additional investigation needed)
- **Hearing officer review:** 60–90 days (if appeal or contested decision)

### Application Fees
- **Initial License:** $200–600 depending on license type
- **Renewal:** $75–300 (typically less than initial)
- **Late renewal:** 10–50% penalty
- **Re-examination fee:** $150–400 (if failing initial exam)

### Document Expiration
- Exam scores: Valid for 3–7 years depending on exam type
- Continuing education certificates: Valid for renewal period (typically 2–3 years)
- Criminal background clearance: Typically valid for 1 year
- Medical certifications (e.g., ECFMG): Valid for entire career (with updates)

## State-Specific Variations

This document provides general guidelines. **Always verify** state-specific requirements:

- Some states have higher exam score minimums or additional tests
- Character & fitness standards vary significantly by state
- Continuing education requirements differ by state and license type
- Reciprocity agreements are state-dependent
- Processing timelines and fees vary

Contact your state licensing board for authoritative requirements.
