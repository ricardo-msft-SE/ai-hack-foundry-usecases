# City of Exampleville — Emergency Response Procedures & Protocols

This document contains official emergency response procedures, evacuation protocols, resource deployment guidelines, and agency responsibilities for the City of Exampleville Office of Emergency Management.

---

## Emergency Classification Levels

| Level | Criteria | Response |
|---|---|---|
| **Level 1 — Monitor** | Weather watch in effect; no immediate threat | OEM monitors; agencies on standby alert |
| **Level 2 — Prepare** | Warning in effect; threat within 48 hours | Pre-positioning begins; shelters on standby |
| **Level 3 — Respond** | Active emergency; life-safety threat | Full activation; evacuations if ordered |
| **Level 4 — Recovery** | Immediate threat resolved; damage present | Shift to recovery operations |

---

## Evacuation Zone System

The city is divided into five evacuation zones, ordered by flood/storm surge risk:

| Zone | Risk Level | Areas Covered | Trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Zone A** | Extreme — evacuate first | Coastal areas, waterfront, low-lying flood plains | Cat 1+ hurricane; major flood watch |
| **Zone B** | High | Near-coastal neighborhoods, 100-year flood plain | Cat 2+ hurricane; flood warning |
| **Zone C** | Moderate | Low-elevation inland areas | Cat 3+ hurricane; major flooding |
| **Zone D** | Low | Most inland residential areas | Cat 4+ hurricane |
| **Zone E** | Minimal | High-elevation areas | Cat 5 hurricane only |

### Evacuation Order Protocol

1. **Voluntary Evacuation Advisory** — issued 72+ hours before projected landfall for Zone A
2. **Mandatory Evacuation Order** — issued 48 hours before for Zone A when sustained winds expected > 58 mph or storm surge > 4 feet
3. Zones B and beyond escalate at 36 and 24 hours respectively
4. Re-entry permitted only after OEM All-Clear declaration (minimum 12 hours post-event)

## Hurricane / Tropical Storm Protocol

### 72+ Hours Before Landfall (Level 1 → Level 2)

- OEM activates Emergency Operations Center (EOC) in monitoring mode
- Public Works inspects and clears storm drains in Zones A and B
- Parks Department secures loose equipment in all parks
- Transit Authority increases bus service to evacuation routes
- Begin public notification via emergency alert system

### 48 Hours Before Landfall (Level 2 → Level 3)

- Issue Mandatory Evacuation Order for Zone A
- Open all designated shelters (minimum 4 primary + 2 special needs)
- Pre-position rescue boats at Fire Stations 1, 3, and 7
- Pre-position generators at critical facilities (hospitals, shelters, water treatment)
- Establish fuel depots at Fire HQ, Police Precinct 2, and Public Works Yard

### 24 Hours Before Landfall (Full Level 3)

- Extend mandatory evacuation to Zone B
- All non-essential city employees dismissed
- Emergency personnel on 12-hour rotating shifts
- Close all bridges rated for less than 75 mph wind when sustained winds reach 50 mph
- Activate mutual aid agreements if resource gap identified

### During Storm

- OEM coordinates all inter-agency communications from EOC
- Fire and EMS respond to life-safety calls only (no property protection)
- Police manage traffic control and enforce evacuation orders
- No aerial operations when winds exceed 35 mph

### Post-Storm (Recovery — Level 4)

- Initial damage assessment: first 6 hours post-storm
- Priority restoration order: (1) Hospitals / emergency services, (2) Water treatment, (3) Schools, (4) Residential
- Re-entry phased: Zone E → Zone D → Zone C → Zone B → Zone A
- Document all actions for FEMA reimbursement

## Flood Response Protocol

### Flash Flood (Rapid Onset — < 6 Hours Warning)

1. Activate emergency alert system immediately
2. Request high-water rescue vehicles from Fire Department
3. Dispatch police to close flooded roads
4. Open nearest shelter to affected area within 2 hours
5. Request mutual aid from neighboring jurisdictions if needed

### River Flooding (Slow Rise — 12–72 Hours Warning)

- Monitor river gauge at City Bridge and Mill Creek stations
- Trigger points:
  - **Watch:** River at 14 feet (flood stage is 17 feet)
  - **Warning:** River at 17 feet
  - **Major Flood:** River at 22 feet (evacuate Zone A low-lying areas)
- Pre-position sandbags at Public Works Yard A (20,000 bags on hand)

## Winter Storm Protocol

### Activation Thresholds

- **Level 1 (Prepare):** Forecast of 4+ inches within 24 hours
- **Level 2 (Respond):** 6+ inches in 24 hours, or ice storm warning
- **Level 3 (Emergency):** 12+ inches, blizzard conditions, or sustained below-zero wind chill

### Snow Removal Priority Routes

| Priority | Route Type | Target Clear Time |
|---|---|---|
| Priority 1 | Emergency routes (hospital access, fire station access) | Within 2 hours of storm end |
| Priority 2 | Main arterials and transit routes | Within 4 hours |
| Priority 3 | Secondary streets | Within 24 hours |
| Priority 4 | Residential streets | Within 48 hours |

### Cold Weather Shelter Activation

- Activate warming centers when wind chill forecast below 15°F
- Minimum: 2 warming centers (north and south)
- Transportation: 311 hotline for rides to warming centers

## Public Health Emergency Protocol

### Disease Outbreak Response

1. City Health Department coordinates with state and CDC
2. OEM provides logistics support (transportation, facility access)
3. Activate Point of Dispensing (POD) sites for mass vaccination or medication distribution
4. POD locations (capacity):
   - Exampleville High School Gymnasium (5,000/day)
   - Community Center North (3,000/day)
   - Fairgrounds (8,000/day)

### Communication Protocol

- All public communications must be approved by Health Department Communications Officer
- Use plain language; avoid medical jargon
- Translate all communications into 5 city languages: English, Spanish, French, Portuguese, Mandarin

## Agency Responsibilities

| Agency | Primary Role | Secondary Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Office of Emergency Management (OEM)** | Coordination, EOC operation, public communications | Mutual aid coordination |
| **Fire Department** | Search and rescue, hazmat response, water rescue | Shelter support |
| **Police Department** | Evacuation enforcement, traffic control, crowd management | Shelter security |
| **Emergency Medical Services (EMS)** | Medical response, ambulance transport | Medical shelter support |
| **Public Works** | Road clearing, infrastructure repair, generator deployment | Water/sewer emergency repairs |
| **Transit Authority** | Evacuation transportation, bus deployment to shelters | Post-storm public transit recovery |
| **Parks Department** | Shelter facility management, debris clearing | Damage assessment |

## Mutual Aid Agreements

The City of Exampleville has active mutual aid agreements with:

- **Neighboring County Emergency Management** — personnel and equipment sharing
- **State Emergency Management Agency** — National Guard activation, state resources
- **Regional Hospital Coalition** — patient surge, medical equipment
- **Regional Transit Authority** — additional evacuation buses
- **Utility Companies** — power restoration crews

### Requesting Mutual Aid

1. OEM Director must authorize all mutual aid requests
2. Document request in the EOC log with timestamp and justification
3. All mutual aid must be tracked for FEMA reimbursement documentation
